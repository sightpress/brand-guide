The SightPress brand guide

Beyond sight.

A powerful CMS for everyone, designed with blind and low-vision creators and audiences in mind. The brand has to look as good as it works, so every colour, typeface and icon here was chosen to be both beautiful and accessible.

The idea: a point of perception

The mark is a single, confident “S” with an amber dot resting in its upper curve, like a pupil in an eye. The dot is the point where content is perceived, whether by eye, by ear through a screen reader, or by touch on a braille display. The S is drawn as one continuous stroke, like a line of text flowing down a page.

Three ideas guide every brand decision:

  • Clear first. Legibility, contrast and plain words come before decoration.
  • Warm, not clinical. Accessibility is the floor, not the ceiling: rich colour, generous space and a characterful display face.
  • Said in words. Nothing is carried by colour or shape alone. Status, warnings and choices always have text.

The motto: “Beyond sight.”

Two words for what SightPress is for: publishing that reaches people however they take it in, by eye, by ear or by touch, and a brand that goes further than how things look. Write it in sentence case with its full stop, which stands for the amber dot. Where the brand palette is on a dark background, the full stop can be amber; on light backgrounds it takes the text colour, because amber is never text on light. Next to the logo, the motto sits under or after the wordmark in the display face, never inside the logo itself.

The motto leads the share cards that preview SightPress links. Keep the motto in English in every locale, like the name; when a translation is needed for meaning, add it as a separate line with its own lang.

Logo

Use the SVG files in logo/. Every file has the accessible name “SightPress”. When the logo is a link, give the link a name that says where it goes, such as “SightPress home”.

Clear space and size

  • Keep clear space around the logo equal to the height of the amber dot multiplied by four (a quarter of the mark’s height).
  • Smallest horizontal logo: 120 CSS pixels wide on screen, 30 mm in print. Below that, use the mark alone.
  • Smallest mark: 16 pixels (favicon). The mark always keeps its rounded tile.

Do

  • Use the primary logo on paper or white, and the dark logo on ink.
  • Use the single-colour logo when only one colour is available.
  • Give it room: generous clear space makes it look confident.

Don’t

  • Recolour the wordmark or the dot, or put the logo on a busy photo.
  • Stretch, rotate, outline or add effects such as shadows or glows.
  • Retype the wordmark in another font; always use the files.

Colour

Iris is the brand: a deep blue-violet, confident without being corporate. Lumen, the amber of the dot, is the spark, used sparingly for highlights and never as text on a light background. Lagoon supports it in illustration and charts. Ink and paper are the neutrals: a warm off-white and a deep blue-black, softer on the eyes than pure black on white.

Use the semantic tokens (--sp-color-text, --sp-color-accent and the rest), not the palette directly. They switch automatically for dark mode and for “increase contrast” settings.

Type

Two families for Latin scripts, plus companions for Arabic and Hebrew, all free under the SIL Open Font License and self-hosted, so there are no third-party requests.

Fraunces

Stories for every reader

Display and headings, 28 pixels and up. A warm, soft serif that gives SightPress its character. Used at weight 640 with the “soft” cut.

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next

Il1 O0 rn m 6b 9g

Body text and interface. Designed with the Braille Institute so that easily confused characters stay distinct for low-vision readers.

Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono

media:01J9Z…

Code, identifiers and tokens.

Scale

A major-third scale from an 18-pixel body size, set in rem so the reader’s browser settings always win. Lines are at most 66 characters long, with 1.6 line spacing.

Arabic and Hebrew

The brand fonts cover Latin scripts only, so Arabic (ar-SA) and Hebrew (he-IL) each get two self-hosted companions, also under the SIL Open Font License, picked to match the brand fonts. They sit right after the brand font in every stack and only download when a page contains their script.

Noto Naskh Arabic, display

قصص لكل قارئ

Headings in Arabic. A classic Naskh, the calligraphic style Arabic readers know from books, playing the part Fraunces plays in Latin.

Noto Sans Arabic, text

انشر لكل قارئ

Body text and interface in Arabic. Open, even letterforms with clear dots, a calm match for Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.

Frank Ruhl Libre, display

סיפורים לכל קורא

Headings in Hebrew. A modern revival of the classic Hebrew book face, with the same warmth as Fraunces.

Noto Sans Hebrew, text

פרסמו לכל קורא

Body text and interface in Hebrew. Clear, open letterforms at the same weights as Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.

  • Mark every Arabic or Hebrew passage with lang and dir, so the right font, direction and screen-reader voice are used.
  • No letter-spacing on Arabic: tracking breaks the joins between letters. Give Arabic headings snug line spacing (1.3), not tight.
  • Neither script has italics. The companions never draw a fake slant; use bold or quotation marks for emphasis instead.
  • Code and identifiers stay in Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono; Arabic or Hebrew inside code uses the text companion.

Other scripts

Every font stack ends with good system and Noto fonts for CJK and Devanagari (and for Arabic and Hebrew, if the self-hosted files fail to load), so non-Latin text never falls back to a mismatched font. Themes self-host the Noto subsets they need.

Icons

54 icons on a 24-pixel grid, drawn with a 2-pixel round stroke in currentColor, so they always match the text beside them and follow forced colours.

  • An icon never stands alone: it sits next to visible text and is hidden from screen readers with aria-hidden="true".
  • If an icon-only button is truly unavoidable, the button gets a full accessible name (“Move ‘Opening hours’ up in Main menu”) and a visible tooltip on focus as well as hover.
  • Direction icons are named “back” and “forward”, not “left” and “right”, and mirror in right-to-left languages.

Social images

Link previews and profile covers for the project’s own accounts and website. They use the dark logo on a deep ink-to-violet background, with the S of the mark drawn large at the inline end and the amber dot glowing. The headline’s full stop is amber too: a small echo of the dot.

  • Always set the alt text. Every image comes with alt text in social/social.json that repeats every word drawn in it, and the build fails if a word is missing. Use it for og:image:alt, twitter:image:alt and the alt field each network offers when you upload.
  • Never put the only copy of something in an image. Dates, links and calls to action go in the post text, where screen readers, translation and search can reach them.
  • Text stays in the safe area. Each network crops covers differently and lays the profile picture over a corner; the build checks that the text fits inside each image’s safe area and never touches the decoration.
  • Sites made with SightPress use their own branding. These images are for SightPress itself, like the logo.

Sound

Many SightPress creators and readers know the product by ear first, so the brand has a voice of its own. Every sound is synthesised by the build from sound/sound.json, in one key (D major pentatonic), from three voices: a soft mallet, a warm bell and a quiet chord. The bell is the sound of the amber dot. There are no third-party recordings.

  • Sound adds to words; it never replaces them. Every interface sound plays alongside a visible message and the same message in a live region. Someone who can’t hear it, or has sounds off, misses nothing.
  • Off until someone turns it on. Interface sounds are a personal setting in the admin, off by default. Public pages never play sound, and nothing plays by itself.
  • Short and quiet, under the screen reader. Interface sounds last at most 1 second and peak at −12 dBFS or lower, so they never cover the screen reader’s voice. The build fails if one is longer or louder.
  • Told apart by shape, not just pitch. Success rises, error falls, warning repeats one note, a notification is one bell. Notes stay between 140 and 1,200 hertz, where most people with hearing loss still hear well, with no harsh highs.
  • The sound logo is for media. Use it to open or close videos, podcasts and talks. Caption it as [SightPress sound logo], so the sound is in the captions and transcript too.

Voice

  • Plain and kind. Short sentences, everyday words, no jargon. “Add alt text”, not “Remediate non-text content”.
  • Say what happened and what to do next. “This page can’t be published yet: 1 image has no alt text.”
  • Full names for actions. “Edit ‘About us’”, never just “Edit”.
  • Confident, never boastful. SightPress is a powerful CMS for everyone; let the work show it.
